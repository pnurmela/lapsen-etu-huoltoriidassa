# Final Publication Architecture for a Finnish Legal and Administrative Report

## Executive summary

The strongest publication model is a **two-tier controlled publication** rather than one report shortened mechanically. The full research report should remain the authoritative evidence base: all 14 substantive chapters and Appendices A–E remain available, but repetition should be edited out and certain high-risk components—legal-source hierarchy, jurisdiction matrices, statutory deadlines, appeal routes, case-law summaries, uncertainty statements and templates—should become **controlled text** that cannot be altered without legal review. The 10–15-page *viranomaisversio* should be a decision-support document, not a miniature treatise.

A release-date legal refresh is necessary even though the manuscript is dated 6 September 2026. In particular, **KHO:2026:62, delivered on 25 August 2026**, is directly relevant to the report because it clarifies the independent procedural standing of a child aged 12 or over in child-protection proceedings. KHO:2026:33 and KHO:2026:45, delivered in May and June 2026, respectively, also clarify appeal restrictions in child-protection matters. These should be checked against Chapters 8, 10 and 13 before publication.

The same recency problem exists in education. OPH's current support-decision guidance was updated in summer 2026 and states that individual support measures require a prompt administrative decision and that appeals concerning the relevant support decision go to the Finnish Supervisory Agency (*Lupa- ja valvontavirasto*, LVV) within 14 days. LVV separately reminded education and early-childhood-education providers in February 2026 to update appeal instructions because matters formerly addressed to the Regional State Administrative Agencies moved to LVV from 1 January 2026.

For publication technology, I recommend **accessible HTML as the canonical living edition**, accompanied by an accessible, tagged PDF, printable one-page operational aids and separately editable DOCX/ODT forms. Finland's Act on the Provision of Digital Services is current in Finlex, while Traficom identifies WCAG 2.1 A and AA criteria as the statutory accessibility baseline for covered digital services and requires covered websites/apps to maintain an accessibility statement.

For governance, use **monthly source surveillance, quarterly legal review, annual full recertification and immediate trigger-based review**. The trigger list should include legislative amendments, new KKO/KHO/ECHR authority, significant EOAK/OKV decisions, LVV/OPH/THL guidance changes, changes to competent authorities or appeal routes, and discovered errors in safety or deadline information. The rapid succession of 2026 LVV, OPH and KHO developments makes this a substantive risk-control measure rather than mere editorial housekeeping.

The analysis below is based on the 14-chapter plus Appendices A–E architecture developed in this conversation. A machine-readable copy of the entire manuscript was not available to re-run paragraph-by-paragraph diffing, so “retain verbatim” below means **retain the approved substantive wording as controlled text, subject only to citation, legal-state and factual corrections before each release**.

## Full research report publication architecture

The full report should remain the **source-of-truth edition**. It should preserve the reasoning needed to understand *why* the practical rules in the short edition are justified. Finlex should remain the primary source for current legislation; KKO/KHO for authoritative domestic case-law interpretation; EOAK for legality-supervision reasoning; and current LVV, OPH and THL materials for operational guidance. Finlex itself describes its legislation collection as updated legislation incorporating amendments into the text.

For editorial control, use three statuses:
* **Controlled-verbatim** means the approved wording is reproduced in the full edition and may be changed only through legal review.  
* **Condense/harmonise** means the argument remains but repetitive exposition should be shortened.  
* **Omit from publication** means internal production notes, drafting history and superseded text do not appear in the public edition.

| Existing report component | Full research report treatment | What must remain controlled-verbatim | What may be condensed or removed | Short-version treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chapter 1 – Summary** | Condense | Final scope statement; legal-status date; neutrality principle | Repetition of Chapter 2 and Executive Summary | Replaced by new executive summary |
| **Chapter 2 – Ten key conclusions** | Retain | The ten final conclusions after legal refresh | Explanatory repetition already given later | All ten reduced to 8–10 operational propositions |
| **Chapter 3 – Concepts and source weight** | Mixed | Definitions of child interest, participation, allegation/observation/assessment; source hierarchy; cautions around “alienation”, “custody harassment”, “procedural abuse” | Long conceptual examples | ½–¾ page source/terminology box |
| **Chapter 4 – Legislative map** | Retain | Statute names, section references, jurisdictional links and caveats | Generic descriptions duplicated elsewhere | Only laws necessary for action tables |
| **Chapter 5 – Authority responsibility matrix** | Retain | Entire final responsibility matrix and “can/cannot decide” boundaries | Narrative surrounding table | One of the principal tables |
| **Chapter 6 – School/ECEC decision matrix** | Retain | Decision authority, hearing rules, information-right distinctions and appeal routes | Historical explanation | One principal matrix |
| **Chapter 7 – Child protection/social welfare pathway** | Retain | Safety triage, statutory timing, child participation, allocation of responsibility and decision points | Descriptive repetition about conflict | One-page pathway + deadlines |
| **Chapter 8 – Case law** | Retain and refresh | Case reference, issue, holding, limiting principle and “what this case does not establish” | Procedural facts irrelevant to the report issue | 4–6 precedent propositions only |
| **Chapter 9 – Lawful/problematic official conduct** | Mixed | Comparison tables and red-flag propositions | Repeated discussion of duties already established in Chapters 4–7 | Compact “do / do not” table |
| **Chapter 10 – Remedies** | Retain | Competent authority, remedy, deadline, effect, appeal/complaint distinction, escalation route | General prose repeated in Chapter 12 | Principal remedies/deadlines table |
| **Chapter 11 – 30-day action plan** | Condense | Safety-first ordering and deadline-protection rules | Day-by-day detail not needed for research readers | Convert to four-stage decision path |
| **Chapter 12 – Templates** | Retain as controlled forms | Each final template, its scope note, legal caveat and routing instruction | Repeated introductory text | Only three micro-templates |
| **Chapter 13 – Open/uncertain questions** | Retain | All uncertainty labels, absence-of-general-rule statements and jurist-referral thresholds | Repetition of settled rules | “Limits and escalation” box |
| **Chapter 14 – Sources** | Retain and maintain dynamically | Primary source bibliography, decision identifiers, dates and source classification | Duplicate URLs/source descriptions | Selected sources only |
| **Appendix A – Practitioner checklist** | Retain | Entire final one-page checklist | Nothing substantive | Include substantially in full |
| **Appendix B – Parent action card** | Retain, clearly audience-labelled | Safety → services → decisions → remedies model | Extended examples | Omit from *viranomaisversio*; publish separately |
| **Appendix C – Decision/deadline table** | Retain and version separately | Deadline fields, authority, remedy and warning that decision-specific instructions control | Repeated general explanations | Integrate core table |
| **Appendix D – Event timeline** | Retain | Source/status/effect/document structure | Long examples | Include six-line mini-template |
| **Appendix E – Evidence/source classification** | Retain | Source taxonomy and prohibition on mechanical evidence scoring | Extended illustrations | Include condensed evidence box |

The most important editorial principle is that **the full edition should not literally freeze change-sensitive legal text forever**. Appeal authorities, statutory deadlines and guidance are exactly the material most likely to become dangerous if copied unchanged. LVV's February 2026 notice concerning the transfer of appeal functions and OPH's summer 2026 support guidance demonstrate this risk.

Before first publication, make the following three items **release blockers**:
1. Chapters 8, 10 and 13 should expressly assess KHO:2026:62.
2. The remedies chapter should cross-check KHO:2026:33 and KHO:2026:45.
3. Every education and ECEC route should use **LVV** where current law assigns LVV competence.

## Precise viranomaisversio specification

The *viranomaisversio* should answer:
> **What must a practitioner identify, assess, decide, document or route correctly in the next stage of this child's case?**

Target: **5,720 words / approximately 37,800 characters including spaces** (≈12–14 substantive pages).

## Prioritisation rules for the short edition

* **Hard source gates first, scoring second:**
  * Highest: Constitution, EU law, ECHR, Acts, decrees
  * Very high: KKO/KHO precedents, ECHR judgments
  * Interpretive: Government proposals (HE)
  * Legality supervision: EOAK / OKV
  * Operational guidance: LVV, OPH, THL, Data Protection Ombudsman
  * Supporting: Reviews, peer-reviewed research, local instructions
* **Scoring criteria (12/19 threshold):** Legal authority (0–5), Decision impact (0–5), Recency/volatility (0–3), Role relevance (0–3), Uncertainty-control value (0–3).
* **Automatic inclusion:** Safety instructions, jurisdiction/competence, statutory or appeal deadlines, and explicit statements of legal uncertainty.

## Publication formats, metadata and distribution

* **Accessible HTML:** Canonical living version.
* **Accessible tagged PDF:** Stable download version.
* **Printable Appendix A:** Practitioner desk aid (A4 single page).
* **Printable Appendix C:** Deadline tracker.
* **Parent action card B:** Distinct standalone audience product.
* **DOCX + ODT:** Editable practical templates.

Versioning scheme: **`MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH + legal-state date`** (e.g. `v1.0.0 — law as at 2026-09-06`).

---

## Update governance and publication approval

* **Monthly:** Scan Finlex, KKO, KHO, EOAK/OKV, LVV, OPH, THL.
* **Quarterly:** Legal editor review, domain reviewer validation.
* **Yearly:** Full source-by-source recertification.
* **Trigger events:** Immediate legal triage upon legislative amendment, controlling precedent, or discovered error in safety/deadline data.

---

## Risk controls and disclaimers

Disclaimers must be embedded in both the full research report and the viranomaisversio, highlighting:
1. General research/practice resource, not individual legal opinion.
2. Verified to specific date; subsequent amendments control.
3. Decision-specific instructions control over general summaries.
4. Descriptive conflict terminology is not a clinical diagnosis or legal proof of causation.
